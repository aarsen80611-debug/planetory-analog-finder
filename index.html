How similarity is computed

  1. Features: roughness, elevation, mean slope, mean temperature, mineralization, UV index, humidity and atmospheric pressure.
  2. Scaling to 0–1: min–max over the whole database, so Earth, Mars and the Moon share one scale. Pressure and humidity are log-transformed by default because they span several orders of magnitude.
  3. Search scope: a Mars or Moon query is compared with Earth sites only. An Earth query uses the bodies chosen in the sidebar.
  4. Missing axes: the Moon has no atmosphere, so a Moon site is compared with Earth on six axes. Coverage is the share of feature weight used.
  5. Metrics (wᵢ are feature weights):
    • Cosine: cos = Σwᵢaᵢbᵢ / (√Σwᵢaᵢ² · √Σwᵢbᵢ²), with a, b = x − 0.5 when centered; similarity = (cos + 1) / 2
    • Euclidean: d = √(Σwᵢ(aᵢ−bᵢ)² / Σwᵢ), similarity = 1 − d
    • Manhattan: d = Σwᵢ|aᵢ−bᵢ| / Σwᵢ, similarity = 1 − d
  6. Score = (α·cosine + β·Euclidean + γ·Manhattan) / (α + β + γ).

Sources for real data

  • Roughness: synthetic aperture radar backscatter σ⁰ from RADARSAT-1 (Canadian open archive) and Sentinel-1 (Copernicus Data Space Ecosystem); Mini-RF for the Moon, SHARAD for Mars.
  • Elevation and slope: Copernicus DEM, MOLA (Mars), LOLA (Moon).
  • Climate: ERA5 (Earth), REMS/MEDA and the Mars Climate Database, Diviner (Moon).
  • Mineralogy: Sentinel-2 and EnMAP, CRISM/OMEGA (Mars), Moon Mineralogy Mapper.
